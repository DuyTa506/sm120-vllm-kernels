## CUTLASS issue and examples

[CUTLASS #3096](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/issues/3096) opened March9,2026 (body report March8), closed by author April14. It describes vLLM0.17/FlashInfer0.6.5 with Qwen3.5-397B-A17B-NVFP4 on four RTX PRO6000s, TP/PP—not our Qwen3.6-35B single-GPU vLLM0.32/B12x path. Later results discuss tile-K64 filtering, shared-memory padding/carveout and tactics. Treat its reported throughput and broken-output diagnosis as historical reproduction-specific evidence, not proof current SM120 native kernels remain broken. The issue itself later records a separate model load-path bug producing similar bad-output symptoms.

[CUTLASS 87a](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/main/examples/87_blackwell_geforce_gemm_blockwise/87a_blackwell_geforce_fp8_bf16_gemm_blockwise.cu) is E4M3 FP8 with blockwise FP32 scaling during K-block accumulation, BF16 output. Its default scale layout follows the128×128×128 tile. It is not an NVFP4 expert replacement and is not automatically interchangeable with our token/channel FP8 dense epilogue contract.

[79a](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/main/examples/79_blackwell_geforce_gemm/79a_blackwell_geforce_nvfp4_bf16_gemm.cu) is SM120 NVFP4→BF16; [79b](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/main/examples/79_blackwell_geforce_gemm/79b_blackwell_geforce_nvfp4_nvfp4_gemm.cu) also generates FP4 output/scales; [79d](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/main/examples/79_blackwell_geforce_gemm/79d_blackwell_geforce_nvfp4_grouped_gemm.cu) is the directly relevant grouped FP4/TMA example. These expose implementation mechanisms, not proof that their unfused examples outperform our routed/gated B12x pipeline. NVIDIA explicitly describes [examples as demonstrations rather than benchmark configurations](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/main/examples/README.md).

## Recipe / Marlin

[Current Qwen recipe](https://github.com/vllm-project/recipes/blob/main/models/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B.yaml) targets `nvidia/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4` for its ModelOpt NVFP4 variant (minimum/image0.28). RTX PRO6000/5090 overrides choose `modelopt_fp4`, Marlin MoE, FlashInfer TRT-LLM attention/block128 and optional MTP4 with draft `flashinfer_cutlass`. This is neither our Unsloth checkpoint format nor our parent attention/MTP2 configuration. The explicit measurement dated August31,2026 in its guide is GB10, not our workstation. Copying the entire recipe would confound checkpoint, activation arithmetic, attention, batching and speculative settings.

The installed vLLM0.32 compressed-tensors NVFP4 scheme calls the NVFP4 backend oracle. That oracle explicitly supports requested Marlin, removes the dynamic-FP4 activation key for A16, converts/repackages NVFP4 weights/scales and returns `nvfp4_w4a16_moe_quant_config`. The [versioned upstream oracle](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/model_executor/layers/fused_moe/oracle/nvfp4.py) and [Marlin FP4 preparation](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.32.0/vllm/model_executor/layers/quantization/utils/marlin_utils_fp4.py) provide the same mechanism. Therefore a same-checkpoint Marlin backend baseline is plausible without re-quantizing/downloading another model, but it changes runtime arithmetic from native W4A4 to W4A16 and may change scale representation/reduction. It is not a bitwise-equivalent kernel substitution. Do not force `--quantization modelopt_fp4` on our compressed-tensors checkpoint.

## Shared expert fusion

[SGLang #33706](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/33706) opened August5,2026 and was automatically closed for inactivity October6; its closed/completed status does not establish a merged implementation. It proposes shared-expert fusion for the official **blockwise FP8** Qwen3.6 checkpoint, shared gate/up N1024/K2048 and down N2048/K512, and explicitly asks for both decode/prefill validation. It is not a demonstrated NVFP4 speedup or a direct vLLM patch.

Our captured `kernels/deepgemm-sm120/results/gdn_actual_capture_round2_1/checkpoint_config.json` confirms `compressed-tensors`, mixed precision: dense attention projections/lm_head are dynamic token FP8 + channel FP8; routed **and shared** experts are NVFP4 group16 with E4M3 scales and local dynamic activation quantization. Hidden2048, intermediate/shared512, experts256/top8. The captured tensor index has shared packed weights, weight/global/input scales; MTP shared weights are separate BF16 tensors. Thus target-model shared/routed formats are compatible in principle, but this is insufficient for fusion: preserve each activation/global scale, the shared sigmoid gate and unchanged top8 route normalization, handle a257th expert and ninth always-on contribution, and include its workspace/scheduling/quantization costs. Never silently use routed FC1 scalar scale for the shared expert if calibration differs.

Installed vLLM0.32 already has model shared-expert fusion plumbing, but `fused_moe/utils.py:55` resolves **AITER** fusion through ROCm availability. It is not an NVIDIA B12x opt-in switch. A source adapter and exact numerical proof are required.

## nickus overlay

[nickus/vllm-sm120](https://github.com/nickus/vllm-sm120) explicitly targets vLLM0.20 and reports measurements July18,2026. Its two overlays reject SM120 in older FlashInfer NVFP4 selectors, allowing native vLLM CUTLASS fallback. It is not a new MMA implementation; the current explicit `flashinfer_b12x` path is different. Its NVFP4 checkpoint is RedHatAI, while the comparison is GPTQ-Marlin, so that comparison changes quantization/checkpoint. Its agentic numbers also have different KV capacity and cache-hit rates. They cannot predict speedup of our same-checkpoint kernel patches, and globally disabling FlashInfer would discard our current native path.
